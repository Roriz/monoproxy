02 Using a guide

Three steps,
while you read.

  1. 1

    Pick where you are

    Open the guide and choose the letter or chapter you're reading right now. You don't need to have finished it.

  2. 2

    Look things up

    The guide covers everything up to the last section you finished. The one you're in stays out, so looking up a name can't spoil the page in front of you.

  3. 3

    Move forward as you go

    When you finish a section, move the picker forward. Your browser remembers your place, and so does the link if you bookmark it. When you reach the end of the book, mark it finished to open everything.

03 Inside a guide

What you've met so far,
in one place.

The panel

The first thing you see after picking your place. It recaps the section you just finished, says who was in it and lists what it left unanswered.

Characters and places

A profile for everyone you've met and every place the story has been, written as of your chapter. Search only finds what you've already read about.

Mysteries

The questions the story has raised so far. An answer shows up only once you've read the chapter that gives it.

Timeline

How the tension rises and falls up to your chapter, with a short recap of each chapter you've read.

Every fact has a source

Each fact names the chapter it comes from, so you can check it against your book. If something looks wrong, you can report it from the guide.

Nothing to install

A guide is a web page, so it works on your phone too. It's free, and you don't sign up or upload a book.

05 Where it comes from

Made from the book,
checked against it.

We prepare each guide from the book's own text with AI help, then review it against that text. Every fact keeps a pointer to the chapter it came from, which is how the guide knows when to show it.

Frankenstein is in the public domain, and its guide isn't affiliated with any of the book's publishers. Guides can get things wrong. When you report a mistake, we fix it.
